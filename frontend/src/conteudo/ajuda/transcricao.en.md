VIGIA Deslizamentos. System user guide.

In November 2008, heavy rains triggered landslides across the Itajaí Valley. VIGIA was created so that the people and the Civil Defense of the 28 municipalities of the Alto Vale do Itajaí (Upper Itajaí Valley) have, in one place, clear information about landslide risk.

Four times a day, the system collects rainfall from the last seven days and the forecast for the days ahead. Using the GeoRisk methodology from Cemaden, it calculates a risk index for each municipality and sorts it into seven levels, from extremely low to extremely high.

When you open VIGIA, you see a map of the 28 municipalities, colored by the day's risk class. At the top, the reference date of the analysis. In the corner of the map, how many municipalities are on alert and when the data was last updated. From the side menu, you can reach every screen in the system.

To see the details, click a municipality, navigate with the keyboard, or look it up by typing its name. The panel shows the risk index, the class, and whether the municipality is on alert. Just below are the numbers behind the calculation: the rain still weighing on the soil, the municipality's critical threshold, and how far it is from reaching it.

From the panel itself, you can share the municipality's status, open a rain simulation, or subscribe to alerts for that municipality on Telegram.

There are seven risk classes. From index 1 up, the municipality goes on alert, and these classes are also hatched on the map. Risk is never shown by color alone: each class has an icon and a name. And with one tap, the colors switch to a palette that works for people with color blindness.

In Forecasts, you see how the risk may change over the next three days. Step through day by day or press play to watch the risk change across the map, day after day. The following days are always marked as estimates.

The map background can be satellite, neutral, streets or terrain, so you can see the land the way you prefer.

In Simulation, you answer one question: what if it rains? Choose the municipality, a period of 24, 48 or 72 hours, and the amount of rain, by typing, dragging, or using the shortcuts from light to extreme. VIGIA uses the same formula as the real index and shows, side by side, the current situation and the simulated one, including whether the municipality could go on alert. You can also simulate the whole region: check regional rain, choose the amount, and simulate again to see all 28 municipalities. Simulation is for planning: it does not change the real data or send alerts.

In Analysis data, you see how the region got here. The indicators summarize the week. The chart shows how the index evolved over 7 or 30 days, or over a period of up to a year, comparing the municipality with the rest of the region. Below, the accumulated rain against the threshold, and a table with the change and trend for each municipality. And the calculation details show the landslide probabilities, the source of the threshold, and the time of each run.

In Risk areas, you see the terrain. High and very high geological risk sectors were mapped by the Geological Survey of Brazil, with the type of movement, the location, and how many people live in each one. Below, the record of rain-related disasters in each municipality, with the official source. And, if you like, the INMET stations that measure rainfall. Remember: the mapping is partial, and there may be risk outside the sectors too.

VIGIA was made for everyone. In the accessibility panel, you choose the language: Portuguese, Spanish or English; the theme, light or dark; and a color palette for color blindness. The whole site can also be used with the keyboard and screen readers, and it remembers your choices.

On your phone, everything works the same way, with every screen one tap away on the bottom bar.

In Help, you'll find this tutorial, with captions and a transcript, tips for each screen, and the link to the project's full documentation.

In the Guide, you learn to recognize warning signs, like new cracks, doors sticking, or muddy water coming out of the slope, and what to do before, during and after heavy rain. You can build your emergency backpack by checking off what you already have, and see what never to do on a slope. Every piece of advice cites its official source.

So you don't depend on opening the site, subscribe to the alerts on Telegram. Tap Start, choose municipalities by region or all at once, and that's it. When one of them reaches alert level, you get a message with the index, the class and the VIGIA link. Use /status to check the situation and /parar to stop. The bot stores only your chat and the chosen municipalities, and the site collects no personal data.

If you need help, your contacts are one tap away. The Contacts screen has the official Santa Catarina Civil Defense dashboard, with rainfall and river levels in real time. Just below, choose your municipality: there are 28, now including Santa Terezinha. Each contact has a phone number, address, directions on the map and the official source.

In an emergency, call 199 for the Civil Defense, 193 for the Fire Department, or 192 for SAMU. VIGIA Deslizamentos: information that helps you decide.
